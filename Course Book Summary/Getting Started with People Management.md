# Course Summary: Getting Started with People Management

I recently completed a course on **People Management** that focuses on the transition from being a successful individual contributor to becoming an effective people manager. The course follows the journey of _Maria_, a high-performing software developer who is promoted to lead a cross-functional product team.

## Key Lessons

### 1. Leadership comes before management

A management title doesn't automatically make someone a leader. Effective managers build trust through:

- Clear communication
- Transparency
- Reliability
- Coaching and mentoring
- Influencing rather than commanding

### 2. The biggest shift is mindset

The most important lesson from the course is changing how success is measured.

| As a Software Developer    | As a People Manager            |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| My success = My work       | My success = My team's success |
| Deliver features           | Enable others to deliver       |
| Solve technical problems   | Remove obstacles for the team  |
| Focus on individual output | Focus on team outcomes         |
| Be the expert              | Develop experts                |

The transition can be summarized as:

> **Developer mindset:** _"How can I solve this problem?"_

> **Manager mindset:** _"How can I help my team solve this problem?"_

Or simply:

> **My Success → My Team's Success**

### 3. Success is measured differently

#### Developer Success

- Delivering projects on time
- Writing quality code
- Fixing production issues quickly
- Technical expertise
- Personal productivity

#### Manager Success

- Team performance
- Employee growth and development
- Employee satisfaction and engagement
- Team health
- Low turnover
- Building future leaders
- Long-term team success

### 4. Build people, not heroes

A manager shouldn't try to solve every problem personally. Instead, they should:

- Delegate meaningful work
- Coach team members
- Empower others to make decisions
- Build leadership capability across the team

### 5. Develop individuals intentionally

The course recommends:

- Creating Individual Development Plans (IDPs)
- Understanding each person's career goals
- Using skill profiles to identify strengths and gaps
- Balancing technical skills with soft skills such as communication, collaboration, and leadership

### 6. Replace assumptions with agreements

Instead of relying on expectations, establish clear agreements that define:

- Who is responsible
- What needs to be done
- When it should be completed
- How progress is communicated
- What success looks like

This significantly reduces misunderstandings and increases accountability.

## My Biggest Takeaway

The most valuable lesson for me was realizing that leadership is less about being the smartest technical person and more about enabling others to succeed.

**As a developer, I was measured by what I delivered.**

**As a manager, I will be measured by what my team delivers, how they grow, and how successful they become.**

**My Success → My Team's Success.**
